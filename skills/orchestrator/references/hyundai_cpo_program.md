# Hyundai Certified Pre-Owned (CPO) Program (as of 2026-05-18)

> **last_verified**: 2026-05-18 (skill stress test iteration 5 + P0-P5 consolidation)
> **Data refresh schedule**: state rates / CPO programs / EV incentives / lease parameters should be re-verified annually or upon any user-cited deal that contradicts. The 2026-05-18 timestamp marks last full audit.

Hyundai CPO is one of the strongest OEM CPO programs in the industry on the **powertrain warranty length axis**, the program inherits Hyundai's "America's Best Warranty" 10yr/100k powertrain proposition and extends it from the original in-service date, which is structurally longer than Subaru (7yr/100k), Honda True Certified (7yr/100k), and Toyota TCUV (7yr/100k).

For Hyundai EVs (Ioniq 5, Ioniq 6, Kona Electric), the CPO also adds a separately-tracked 10yr/100k high-voltage battery component coverage line, the strongest single EV-CPO line item among non-luxury brands.

This file mirrors the structure of `subaru_cpo_program.md`, `honda_cpo_program.md`, and `toyota_cpo_program.md`.

Genesis CPO (luxury) is a separate program and is covered in `genesis_cpo_program.md` (P3.5 deliverable).

## Eligibility Criteria

Per Hyundai Certified Pre-Owned at hyundaiusa.com/us/en/certified:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| Age from original in-service date | **Max 7 years** (e.g., 2026 program covers 2019-2026 in-service vehicles) |
| Mileage | **Under 80,000 mi** |
| Inspection | Must pass Hyundai's **173-point certified inspection** |
| Dealer | Must be sold by authorized Hyundai dealer |
| Title / history | Clean title; no major accident on CARFAX / AutoCheck; no flood, salvage, fire, or rebuilt branding |

A 2022 Hyundai Tucson SEL with 36,000 mi sold by a Hyundai dealer in 2026 is fully eligible (4 yr / 36k mi, solidly inside both windows).

A 2020 Hyundai Sonata with 76,000 mi in 2026 is at the eligibility edge: 6 yr from in-service, under 80k mi, eligible. A 2019 Sonata with 78,000 mi in 2026 is also at the edge (7 yr from in-service depending on month), still eligible by mileage; verify the in-service date.

A 2018 Hyundai in 2026 is generally NOT CPO eligible (>7 years from in-service).

### Single-tier program

Hyundai CPO is a single tier, unlike Honda True Certified's two-tier (Standard + Certified+) split. All eligible Hyundai units get the same coverage package.

## Coverage Granted (When Enrolled)

### ICE / Hybrid models

| Coverage | Term |
|----------|------|
| **Powertrain warranty extension** | **10 years / 100,000 miles from original in-service date** (key structural standout, longest in industry alongside Kia) |
| **Limited bumper-to-bumper extension** | **1 year / 12,000 miles** added after factory 5yr/60k B2B expires |
| Deductible | $0 |
| Roadside Assistance | 10 years / unlimited mi 24/7 included (from original in-service date) |
| Transferable | Yes (to subsequent buyers within warranty period) |
| Trip Interruption | $200/day, up to $1,000 total |
| Rental Car Allowance | $35/day for covered repairs (max 10 days) |
| Sirius XM trial | 90-day on enrollment |

The 10yr/100k powertrain coverage starts from the **original in-service date**, NOT the CPO purchase date. So a 2022 Hyundai that went into service in 2022 has CPO powertrain coverage through 2032 (10 years) or 100,000 mi, whichever comes first.

### EV models (Ioniq 5 / Ioniq 6 / Kona Electric)

EV models get the standard ICE/Hybrid coverage PLUS a separate high-voltage battery component coverage line:

| Coverage | Term |
|----------|------|
| **High-voltage battery component coverage** | **10 years / 100,000 miles from original in-service date** |
| Battery degradation threshold | If pack falls below 70% state-of-health within 10yr/100k window, Hyundai covers replacement |
| Onboard charger / inverter / electric motor | Covered under HV battery component line |
| Reduction gear (EV transaxle) | Covered under powertrain warranty (10yr/100k) |

This is the strongest single OEM-CPO line item for EV buyers among non-luxury brands. Out-of-warranty Ioniq 5 / Ioniq 6 battery replacement runs **$15,000-$22,000** at a Hyundai dealer (the pack is ~$11k-$15k parts + labor + recalibration). The 10yr/100k CPO coverage on a 3-4 year old Ioniq 5 with 30-50k mi has very real residual value, buyer essentially gets ~6-7 more years of HV battery protection for free.

## Embedded Value

Compare Hyundai CPO to third-party extended powertrain warranties:

| Coverage Source | Cost |
|-----------------|------|
| Hyundai CPO (built-in) | Already in dealer price |
| Hyundai Protection Plan VSA (separate, post-CPO) | $1,500-$2,800 layered on top |
| Third-party (CARCHEX, Endurance, Olive) | $2,000-$3,000 for equivalent 10/100k powertrain |
| Third-party EV-battery-inclusive coverage | $3,500-$5,500 (very limited provider availability) |

**Hyundai CPO is functionally a $1,500-$2,500 embedded value for ICE/Hybrid models** when the dealer enrolls it as part of the sale. The long powertrain term (10/100k from in-service) is the structural driver, third-party equivalents at that length are notably more expensive than 7/100k plans.

**For EV models (Ioniq 5/6, Kona Electric), embedded value rises to $3,000-$4,500** because the 10yr/100k HV battery coverage has very limited third-party equivalent at reasonable cost.

**Market premium typical**: **$800-$1,500 over identical non-CPO same VIN** at typical US Hyundai dealers. EV CPO premium often runs an additional $300-$700, but this premium captures real HV-battery residual value, not just dealer margin theater.

So buyer often nets **+$700 to +$1,500 of value** (ICE/Hybrid) or **+$2,000 to +$3,500** (EV) after paying the CPO premium.

## Hyundai SmartSense ADAS Recalibration (post-accident or CPO restoration)

Hyundai SmartSense bundles the brand's ADAS suite (Forward Collision-Avoidance, Lane Keep, Smart Cruise, Highway Drive Assist on higher trims). When CPO inspection finds a prior accident or camera/radar removal, the 173-point inspection requires recalibration before CPO certification.

Typical Hyundai SmartSense recal costs (dealer service department):

| Job | Cost |
|---|---|
| Front radar recal (after bumper replacement) | $400-$700 |
| Front camera (windshield-mounted) recal | $250-$450 |
| Blind Spot Collision-Avoidance radar recal (after rear quarter repair) | $350-$600 |
| Full SmartSense suite recal (after multi-zone accident) | $800-$1,200 |

If the CPO certificate shows a prior accident with bumper or quarter panel work, verify SmartSense recal was performed and documented, sometimes a non-Hyundai body shop will replace parts without forwarding the vehicle for OEM recalibration. A non-recalibrated SmartSense system may show no fault codes but mis-track, which is a safety issue and a CPO disqualifier if discovered after sale.

## What Hyundai CPO Does NOT Cover

- Wear items (tires, brake pads, wiper blades, 12V battery)
- Accident damage (not warranty work)
- Cosmetic items (paint chips, interior wear, scratched alloys)
- Items modified or altered by aftermarket parts (tunes, lifts, non-Hyundai audio)
- Routine maintenance after factory term (no included maintenance under CPO, separate Hyundai Complimentary Maintenance covers 3yr/36k on NEW only)
- Pre-existing conditions documented at time of CPO inspection
- 12V auxiliary battery on EVs (only the HV traction pack is covered under EV battery line)

## CPO vs Non-CPO Math (worked example, Ioniq 5 EV)

For a 2022 Ioniq 5 SEL AWD 32k mi:

| Scenario | Effective Cost |
|----------|---------------|
| **Hyundai CPO included** in dealer sale at $33,990 | $33,990 OTD basis (CPO + 10/100k powertrain + 10/100k HV battery embedded) |
| Non-CPO same VIN at $32,490 (-$1,500) + buy Hyundai Protection Plan VSA $2,000 + EV-battery third-party rider $2,500 | $36,990 effective |
| Non-CPO same VIN at $32,490 + third-party 7/100k powertrain only $2,200 (no EV battery coverage) | $34,690 effective with HV battery risk uncovered ($15k-$22k expected-value exposure) |
| Non-CPO same VIN at $32,490 + self-insure (accept warranty + HV battery risk) | $32,490 net cost, accept ~$5,000-$8,000 of expected-value risk on HV battery + powertrain combined |

A non-CPO Hyundai EV priced $1,500 below the CPO equivalent is roughly break-even on ICE-equivalent coverage, but the HV battery coverage gap means it's effectively WORSE for EV buyers self-insuring against the $15k-$22k battery replacement event. **For Hyundai EV models (Ioniq 5/6, Kona Electric), CPO is structurally hard to beat with self-insure math.**

For ICE / Hybrid Hyundai (Tucson, Santa Fe, Sonata, Elantra, Palisade): CPO wins if the dealer charges less than ~$1,200 premium over the non-CPO equivalent.

## Negotiating Hyundai CPO (Phase 6 play)

If a Hyundai is CPO-eligible but the dealer has not enrolled it:

> Could you enroll this vehicle in the Hyundai Certified Pre-Owned program as part of the sale? This adds the 10yr/100k powertrain warranty extension, 1yr/12k limited B2B, and (for EV models) the 10yr/100k high-voltage battery coverage. Standard Hyundai-authorized dealer offering for sub-80k mi cars under 7 years from in-service.

Dealers can enroll a qualifying car for **~$500-$800 internal cost** (173-point inspection + program enrollment fee). They may accept this as part of the deal at no additional charge, especially if OTD ask has been reduced elsewhere.

If the dealer charges separately for CPO enrollment ($1,000-$1,500), it's usually still better value than buying third-party post-sale, especially for EV models where third-party HV battery coverage is expensive and limited.

**Phase 6 negotiation play**: When a non-CPO 2020+ Ioniq 5 / Ioniq 6 / Kona Electric is priced within $1,500 of a CPO same-trim same-mileage comp, ask the non-CPO dealer to enroll CPO at their cost as part of the deal. The HV battery coverage delta makes this the single highest-leverage margin give on Hyundai EV used-car negotiations.

## For Out-of-CPO Cars (Over 7 Years or 80k+ mi)

A 2018 Hyundai or any Hyundai with 85k+ mi is ineligible for CPO. Options:

1. **Hyundai Protection Plan (VSA)**, Hyundai's separately-sold extended warranty. Plans 5/60k, 7/100k, 10/100k from time of purchase. For EV models, Hyundai offers a separate EV Protection Plan tier covering HV battery components, currently the only OEM-grade extended battery warranty available post-CPO.
   Typical Hyundai dealer Protection Plan pricing for an out-of-CPO 2018 Tucson (~80k mi at purchase):
   - Powertrain only: approximately $1,800-$2,500
   - Comprehensive B2B: approximately $2,800-$3,800
   - Comprehensive B2B + EV battery (EV only): approximately $4,000-$5,500 (EV battery rider is $1,200-$1,800 additional)
2. **Third-party extended warranty**, buy elsewhere (CARCHEX / Endurance / Olive). For Hyundai EVs, third-party HV battery coverage is very limited, verify the plan explicitly covers high-voltage traction battery, inverter, and onboard charger. Most generic "powertrain" third-party plans EXCLUDE HV battery components.
3. **Self-insure**, accept warranty risk. Hyundai's reliability has improved markedly since 2020 (Theta II engine recall era cars are mostly aging out). For 2020+ ICE Hyundai with clean maintenance history, self-insure is defensible. **NOT recommended for out-of-warranty Hyundai EV**, the HV battery replacement event is the structural risk that's hard to self-insure against.

For out-of-CPO ICE Hyundai, expect price to be ~$1,200 lower than a comparable CPO car. For out-of-CPO Hyundai EV, expect ~$2,500-$4,000 lower (lose HV battery coverage in addition).

## CPO Verification at Close

Confirm CPO status before signing:

- [ ] **Hyundai Certified Pre-Owned Certificate** (PDF) with VIN, vehicle data, in-service date
- [ ] **173-point inspection report** (signed by Hyundai-certified tech, dated)
- [ ] Verify warranty start date matches original in-service date
- [ ] Confirm transferability documentation
- [ ] For EV models: verify HV battery component coverage 10yr/100k is explicitly noted on the CPO certificate (separate line from powertrain)
- [ ] Verify SmartSense ADAS recalibration documentation if prior accident exists
- [ ] If dealer says "this is CPO" but cannot produce certificate at close: walk or demand documentation before signing

Without these in hand, "CPO" claims are unverified and the buyer may be paying CPO pricing for non-CPO coverage.

## Brand-Specific Model Notes

### Tucson (4th gen 2022+) / Santa Fe (4th gen 2024+)
- Most common Hyundai compact / mid-size SUV on used lots
- Hybrid trims (Tucson Hybrid / Santa Fe Hybrid) qualify for standard 10yr/100k powertrain, but Hyundai does NOT add a separate hybrid component line (unlike Toyota TCUV). Hybrid battery is rolled into the powertrain line.
- AWD HTRAC adds ~$1,500-$2,000 over FWD same trim, year, miles

### Elantra (7th gen 2021+) / Sonata (8th gen 2020+)
- Sedan staples; reliable but compete with Honda Civic / Toyota Corolla on resale
- Sonata Hybrid (2020+) qualifies for 10/100k coverage; standard powertrain treatment

### Palisade (1st gen 2020+) / Santa Cruz (2022+)
- Palisade is the 3-row family SUV; Santa Cruz is the small pickup. Both qualify for standard CPO.
- Palisade is the most-trim-stratified Hyundai, SE / SEL / Limited / Calligraphy / XRT; verify trim badging matches advertised price ($5k-$10k spread)

### Ioniq 5 (2022+) / Ioniq 6 (2023+) / Kona Electric (2018+)
- EV models, qualify for the EV-specific 10yr/100k HV battery component coverage
- Ioniq 5 N (2024+) high-performance trim qualifies but has different battery thermal management, verify N-specific service items
- 800V architecture on Ioniq 5/6, supports 250kW+ DC fast charging
- Kona Electric (2018-2024) has 400V architecture; smaller pack (~64kWh)
- See `ev_buyer_playbook.md` for SoH check protocol and NACS adapter notes

### N-line / N performance models (Elantra N, Kona N, Veloster N retired 2022)
- Higher-performance trims; CPO still applies but verify aftermarket-tune-clean before purchase (CPO disqualifies tuned vehicles)

## Related Hyundai Programs

- **Hyundai Protection Plan (HPP)**, Hyundai's separately-sold extended warranty / VSA. Different from CPO.
- **Hyundai Complimentary Maintenance**, included free maintenance (oil changes, tire rotations, multi-point inspection) for 3yr/36k on NEW vehicles; does NOT transfer to CPO buyers
- **Hyundai Owner Assurance**, return Hyundai customer loyalty program; typically $500-$1,000 off MSRP on new
- **Hyundai College Graduate / Military Programs**, $400-$500 off new
- **Hyundai Drive, Subscription**, discontinued 2023; not active on used market

Most of these apply to NEW Hyundai purchases. Used Hyundai CPO is its own thing, layered on top of the standard used-car sale.

## Hyundai CPO vs Toyota TCUV vs Honda CPO vs Subaru CPO, Quick Comparison

| Feature | Hyundai CPO | Toyota TCUV | Honda True Certified | Subaru CPO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Age cap | **7 yr from in-service** (longest) | 6 yr from in-service | 6 yr from in-service | 5 yr (current year + 4 preceding) |
| Mileage cap | 80k mi | 85k mi | 85k mi | 80k mi |
| Inspection | **173-point** (most thorough) | 160-point | 150-point | 152-point |
| Powertrain extension | **10 yr / 100k from in-service** (longest in industry) | 7 yr / 100k from in-service | 7 yr / 100k from in-service | 7 yr / 100k from in-service |
| Limited B2B post-factory | 1 yr / 12k | 1 yr / 12k | 1 yr / 12k or 2 yr / 24k (Certified+) | None as standard CPO |
| Hybrid Component coverage | n/a (rolled into powertrain) | **10 yr / 150k (Hybrid/PHEV only)** | n/a | n/a |
| **EV HV battery coverage** | **10 yr / 100k (EV only)**, unique to Hyundai (and Kia) among non-luxury | n/a (separate bZ4X BEV warranty applies) | n/a | n/a (no Subaru BEV at CPO depth, Solterra deferred to P3.5) |
| Embedded value (ICE/Hybrid) | $1,500-$2,500 | $1,000-$2,000 ICE / $2,500-$4,000 Hybrid | $1,200-$1,800 | $2,000-$2,500 |
| Embedded value (EV) | **$3,000-$4,500** | n/a (TCUV does not extend BEV battery) | n/a | n/a |
| Market premium typical | $800-$1,500 (EV +$300-$700) | $1,000-$2,000 (Hybrid +$300-$700) | $800-$1,500 | $1,500-$2,500 |
| Tier structure | Single tier | Single tier | Two-tier | Single tier |

**Key structural difference**: Hyundai CPO's 10yr/100k powertrain term from original in-service is the longest among non-luxury brands. For EV buyers, the 10yr/100k HV battery component coverage is the strongest OEM-CPO EV line item across non-luxury brands. For a Tucson / Santa Fe / Palisade buyer, Hyundai CPO is comparable to Toyota TCUV on ICE; for an Ioniq 5 / Ioniq 6 buyer, Hyundai CPO is structurally hard to beat.
